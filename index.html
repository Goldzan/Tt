Water

A contour line is a level set — every point on it is at the same height — so a wave written in height alone crests exactly along one. Each contour is the top of a ripple, and the water falls away to a trough halfway to the next. Lit from the upper left; a still picture, not an animation.

How many ripples there are is how many contours there are, on the Terrain panel above.

How raised the waves are, and whether they read as a long clean swell or as a chop with edges on it. Neither moves a crest off its contour.

How much the height still shows in the colour. At zero the water is one colour everywhere and only the light shapes it; turn it up and the valleys go deep and the summits pale, the way the other designs read the ground.

How hard the sun comes off the swell. The surface fills the map area, so a transparent background shows only in the margin.

Realistic water

Clarity moves two things at once: how far the ground below slides as a wave passes over it, and how brightly each crest gathers the light going through it into a band underneath.

Seen from directly above there is no horizon in the picture to take a sky from, so it is given here. The flattest water mirrors what is overhead; the steepest flanks catch the horizon.

T-shirt

Choose T-shirt front or T-shirt back above the picture to see the map printed on a shirt.

Drag on the shirt to move the print. It stays inside the shirt's print area, which is outlined while you drag. The front and the back each keep their own place and size.

At 100% the print is as large as its shape allows in the print area. Across and down run from one edge of the area to the other, so a print that already spans it one way has nowhere to go that way.

Text lines

Words 1 phrase

One phrase per line: the first goes on the lowest contour, the next on the one above, and the list repeats up the map. {place}, {elevation} (metres), {feet} and {index} are filled in for you.

A line too short to hold its phrase is left blank rather than lettered with part of a word.
